**Detailed assessment of the Challenge**

The correspondence between its concrete Hilbert coordinates and the paper's polynomials is

\[
 U\psi=\sum_{\sigma:\Lambda\to\{0,\ldots,4\}}\psi_\sigma
       \prod_{x\in\Lambda}\sqrt{\binom4{\sigma_x}}
                 z_{x,1}^{4-\sigma_x}z_{x,2}^{\sigma_x}.
\]

This is a complex-linear isometry: the squared norm of each monomial in `d:poly` is the inverse binomial coefficient. Thus the standard coordinate inner product is exactly the tensor-product inner product under this identification, not an unweighted monomial inner product.

- **Sizes and lattice.** `Size = {n : ℕ // 3 ≤ n}` is exactly the positive-integer range in the paper. Passing from integers at least three to these naturals loses no periods. `ZMod 0` would be the integers in Mathlib, but it is unreachable here; every site type is finite and nonempty. The site count is `LN`. Both directions in `Fin 2` occur. `nextSite` performs modular addition in the appropriate coordinate, not ordinary addition followed by a partial indexing operation.
- **Edges, including period three.** The map `(x,d) ↦ {x,nextSite x d}` bijects the `2LN` bond indices with the simple unoriented nearest-neighbor edges. A loop would make a period divide one. Reversing a positive edge to get another positive edge in the same coordinate would make that period divide two. Horizontal/vertical equality would require a coordinate step to vanish. None can occur for periods at least three. In a three-cycle, the three positive edges are still three different unordered pairs. There are four distinct neighbors and four incident edges per site. Orienting the singlets this way is one of the paper's permitted orientation choices.
- **Spin matrices and scalar types.** In `spinZ`, subtraction takes place in ℂ, so the five entries really are `2,1,0,−1,−2`. In `spinPlus`, the `Fin` values are first read as naturals: the condition is `i.val+1=j.val`, not modular `Fin` addition. Its four nonzero entries are `2,√6,√6,2`. The natural subtraction `5−j.val` is safe since `j.val≤4`; no negative square root or boundary wrap occurs. `spinMinus` is the complex conjugate transpose. The scalar `1/2` in the Casimir is complex division, not natural division. Endomorphism multiplication is composition.
- **Relation to the polynomial action.** In monomial coordinates these matrices are `J₊=z₁∂₂`, `J₋=z₂∂₁`, `J_z=(z₁∂₁−z₂∂₂)/2`. The paper explicitly gives the first two differential operators in `sec3-quantum-frames.tex:49–60`. For its action `p(z) ↦ p(g⁻¹z)`, differentiation at `iσ_a/2` gives `i(−J_x,J_y,−J_z)`, with `J_x=(J₊+J₋)/2`, `J_y=(J₊−J₋)/(2i)`. This is a common rotation of the spin axes and preserves the total Casimir exactly. In particular, the sign convention for the diagonal weights does not change the spin-four space or the Hamiltonian. The code is not assuming a different monomial norm or a different coupling.
- **Tensor lifting.** The matrix in `atSite` has entries `A(σ_x,τ_x) ∏_{y≠x}δ_{σ_y,τ_y}`. Mathlib's matrix conversion acts by ordinary matrix-vector multiplication, with row index the output coordinate. Thus `onBond` is the sum of the two endpoint operators, with identities on every spectator factor.
- **Projection.** The single-site Casimir is `6I`. For a two-site pair, the total Casimir has eigenspaces of dimensions `1,3,5,7,9` at eigenvalues `0,2,6,12,20`. Therefore the code selects precisely total spin four. Independently, let `Δ:V₄⊗V₄→V₈` be diagonal polynomial evaluation, as in the paper's proof of `l:edge`. In orthonormal coordinates,
  \[
   \Delta_{k,(i,j)}=\mathbf1_{k=i+j}\sqrt{\binom4i\binom4j/\binom8k},
   \qquad \Delta\Delta^*=I,
  \]
  and its range-adjoint projection `Δ*Δ` equals
  \[
   \frac{C(C-2I)(C-6I)(C-12I)}{20\cdot18\cdot14\cdot8},
  \]
  where `C` is the pair Casimir. I checked the eigenspace dimensions and this matrix identity in exact rational arithmetic after changing to monomial coordinates. This identifies the code's projection with the paper's projection, independently of its doc comment. The global range has dimension `9·5^(LN−2)`.
- **Hamiltonian and normalization.** `starProjection` is an orthogonal projection onto the stated submodule, not a choice of an eigenvector or an arbitrary idempotent. Its conversion to a linear map changes no action. `H` is the sum of these projections with coefficient one over the above bijective bond index set. There is no factor `1/2`, volume normalization, edge multiplicity, or shifted ground energy. Each projection is positive and self-adjoint, hence so is `H`.
- **The actual state.** `singlet` is exactly `z_{x,1}z_{y,2}−z_{x,2}z_{y,1}` with zero-based variable indices. No normalized-singlet factor has been inserted. Every factor is a nonzero polynomial because its endpoints are distinct. Their product is nonzero in the polynomial ring over ℂ. Each site occurs in four factors, so the product is homogeneous of degree four separately at every site. `monomialDegree` lists every basis monomial of precisely this multidegree. Its subtraction `4−σ_x` is safe. The binomial coefficients in `B` are `1,4,6,4,1`; every square-root denominator is positive. Dividing coefficients by their product is exactly the inverse of `U` above. Consequently the coefficient extraction cannot turn this nonzero polynomial into zero. Reversing chosen edge orientations changes `B` only by an overall sign, which has no effect on nonvanishing or its span.
- **Ground space.** The first conjunct asserts both `B ≠ 0` and the equality of the full kernel with the complex linear span of the singleton `{B}` for **every** pair of allowed periods. It is not merely membership in the kernel, existence of some ground state, or eventual uniqueness. Positivity of `H` makes its kernel the zero-energy ground space, and the explicit nonvanishing clause makes that space one-dimensional.
- **Eigenvalues and gap.** Mathlib's `HasEigenvalue μ` means a nonzero eigenspace, equivalently existence of a **nonzero** vector with `Hψ=μψ`. It does not allow the zero vector to witness arbitrary eigenvalues. Although μ is quantified in ℝ and then cast into ℂ, self-adjointness ensures every complex eigenvalue is real. Positivity ensures all nonzero eigenvalues are positive. Thus the clause is exactly a lower bound for the paper's smallest positive eigenvalue. It is not vacuous: the tensor with every local coordinate `k=0` is fixed by every bond projection and has Hamiltonian eigenvalue `2LN>0`.
- **Quantifier order and constants.** One real `c>0` and one natural threshold `L₀≥3` work for all `L,N` above that threshold and all their nonzero eigenvalues. Neither constant depends on the periods, parity, aspect ratio, or eigenvalue. There is no upper bound or comparability condition on `L/N`. For any threshold, allowed periods exist, including both parities. The paper's main statement supplies no explicit numeric `c` or `L₀`; neither does Lean. The constants used to prove their existence belong to the input statements, not to the headline.
