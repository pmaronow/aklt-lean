The paper's definition is the sum of the orthogonal total-spin-four projections, once per simple unoriented nearest-neighbour edge, for all integers `L,N ≥ 3` (sec1-result.tex:7–27).

The frozen definitions implement that domain and edge set (AKLT/Challenge/Main.lean:16–39). Each bond chooses a site and one of the two positive coordinate directions. There are no loops since neither period divides one. Two positive indices could describe one unordered edge with reversed endpoints only if a period divided two, or if different coordinate directions produced a zero unit displacement. Both are excluded by `L,N ≥ 3`. Every unordered nearest-neighbour edge has one such index. Thus there are exactly `2LN` bonds, including when a period is three; the Hamiltonian has coefficient one per edge, with no parity restriction or size threshold.

The projector construction also agrees, independently of its docstrings. `atSite` is the single-site matrix with Kronecker deltas on all spectator coordinates; `Matrix.toEuclideanLin` acts by matrix times a column vector (Mathlib/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/PiL2.lean:1242–1247; Mathlib/Analysis/Normed/Lp/Matrix.lean:30–47). In the orthonormal polynomial basis, `spinZ` has entries `2-j`, and `spinPlus e_j = sqrt(j(5-j)) e_(j-1)`; `spinMinus` is its adjoint. The first subtraction is in ℂ, so negative magnetic labels are preserved; the natural subtraction in `5-j` never truncates on `Fin 5`. `conjTranspose` conjugates and swaps matrix indices (Mathlib/LinearAlgebra/Matrix/ConjTranspose.lean:133–136). Multiplication of `Module.End` means composition, not entrywise multiplication (Mathlib/Algebra/Module/LinearMap/End.lean:47–59). Hence `bondCasimir` is exactly `Z_total² + (U_total D_total + D_total U_total)/2`.

The inverse-action convention was checked against sec3-quantum-frames.tex:38–64. Although the paper's diagonal weights are `j-2`, its displayed infinitesimal generators are `U-D` and `-i(U+D)`, with diagonal generator `-iZ`. Spectator coordinates remain identities throughout.

`Module.End.eigenspace C 20` means the ordinary kernel of `C - 20 • 1` (Mathlib/LinearAlgebra/Eigenspace/Basic.lean:421–429). `Submodule.starProjection` is the orthogonal projection onto that subspace, with exactly that range and its orthogonal complement as kernel (Mathlib/Analysis/InnerProductSpace/Projection/Basic.lean:120–133, :303–307, :336–340). The paper specifies the unnormalised product of oriented edge determinants (sec1-result.tex:13–18, :27–28), as a vector in the polynomial Hilbert space fixed by sec3-quantum-frames.tex:14–18 and :74–76.

The underlying `singlet` is exactly `z_(x,1) z_(y,2) - z_(x,2) z_(y,1)`, with zero-based coordinate names and the permitted choice of positive-coordinate orientation (AKLT/Challenge/Main.lean:102–115). Choosing this particular orientation is allowed by the paper's instruction to fix one orientation per edge; reversing choices changes only the prescribed overall sign, as discussed at sec3-quantum-frames.tex:218–220.

`MvPolynomial` is the ordinary polynomial algebra, with finitely supported natural exponent vectors and ordinary coefficients; `X` is a degree-one variable and multiplication adds exponents (Mathlib/Algebra/MvPolynomial/Basic.lean:80–106, :125–127, :652–654). `Finsupp.equivFunOnFinite.symm` merely packages the supplied exponent function as a finitely supported function (Mathlib/Data/Finsupp/Defs.lean:204–217). Therefore `monomialDegree σ` records exactly the exponents `(4-σ(x), σ(x))` at each site, without a permutation, sign, or factorial factor.

Every site has four distinct incident bonds, even at period three, and each determinant contributes degree one at each endpoint. Hence the determinant product has degree four at each site and all its monomials are captured by these configuration indices. This agrees with the paper's explicit degree argument (sec3-quantum-frames.tex:147–152, :198–205). The coefficient extraction in `B` loses no part of the polynomial.

Finally, dividing each ordinary monomial coefficient by `Π_x sqrt(choose(4,σ(x)))` is exactly the conversion to the tensor orthonormal basis used by `Hilbert` (AKLT/Challenge/Main.lean:117–129). All factors are strictly positive before embedding in ℂ, so the finite product is nonzero; totalised division cannot silently erase coefficients. `WithLp.toLp 2` only wraps the coordinate function and preserves each value (Mathlib/Analysis/Normed/Lp/WithLp.lean:50–56, :97; Mathlib/Analysis/Normed/Lp/PiLp.lean:141). In particular, applying `physicalEval` to `vbs L N` evaluates exactly the product of determinants. There is no global normalisation or extra scale. Distinct endpoint variables make every determinant nonzero, so the polynomial integral-domain argument also excludes a zero VBS vector, as intended in sec3-quantum-frames.tex:198–205.
