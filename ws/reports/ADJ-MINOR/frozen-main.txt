  THE SPECTRAL GAP OF THE SPIN-2 AKLT MODEL ON THE SQUARE
                          LATTICE

       Abstract. We prove that the spin-2 AKLT Hamiltonian on the L × N square-lattice torus has
       a unique ground state and a spectral gap bounded below uniformly in all even L, N larger than a
       fixed constant. The proof is computer-assisted. A finite family of local inequalities is verified by
       computer in exact integer and rational arithmetic, and two bounds on weighted sums over lattice
       trails in floating-point arithmetic with upward rounding. The proof replaces each spin by a finite
       tight frame of coherent states, which turns the quantum problem into the spectral gap of a heat-bath
       dynamics for a finite-state Markov field. A finite-size criterion and the theory of Martinelli, Olivieri
       and Schonmann reduce this gap to a coupling estimate for window marginals in large squares. We
       prove that estimate with an exact factorization over a separating cut, which converts it into bounds
       on pair correlations next to removed sites, and these bounds are obtained from a walk expansion
       and a linear recursion whose kernel is controlled by computer-assisted estimates.

   The proof of Theorem 1.1 is computer-assisted. Three statements are verified by computer,
all at the pair of constants (cs , ct ) = (0.961, 0.935) (Theorem 8.1). Hypothesis (H1) is a list of
7,962 inequalities, one for each local configuration (“pattern”) in an explicit class Pext of radius-2
neighbourhoods of the square lattice. Each inequality asserts that a ratio of two functions of at
most twenty unit vectors in R3 , affine in each vector, is at least cs or ct on the whole product of
spheres. We verify it in exact integer and rational arithmetic, with tests of positive definiteness
of integer matrices for each pattern, one for each exchange-symmetry sector of the pattern (§8.3
and Appendix D); the smallest margins are 2.4 · 10−4 and 5.6 · 10−4 . Hypothesis (H2), a bound
on the growth of weighted trail sums, and Hypothesis (H3′ ), a bound on the spectral radius of a
2 × 2 kernel built from closed trails, are verified in floating-point arithmetic with upward rounding
(§8.1, §8.2 and Appendix C); the bound for (H2) was also reproduced in exact rational arithmetic.
These two hypotheses use (H1) only through the two numbers cs and ct . Everything else is proved
here or cited with a precise locator. One cited result, [9, Theorem 4.1] as it is invoked in [10], is
applied to a finite single-spin space larger than the two-valued one for which its proof is written
out; Remark 4.5 explains why the proof applies. The constants c and L0 are not explicit.

1.2. Structure of the proof. The proof is a chain of five reductions. We describe them in the
order in which they are used; each is proved in the section indicated, and §4 assembles the proof of
Theorem 1.1.
   From the quantum gap to a classical heat bath (§2). Replace each spin by the twelve vertices of
an icosahedron on one sublattice and the twenty vertices of a dodecahedron on the other. Both are
spherical 5-designs, so the corresponding spin-2 coherent states form tight frames, and the frame
map is an isometry from HΛ into a space of functions of a finite-state configuration. The ground
state B becomes a nowhere-vanishing function whose square is a nearest-neighbour Gibbs measure
µD with strictly positive weights. Theorem 2.5 shows that gap(HL,N ) is bounded below by a fixed
multiple of the spectral gap of the single-site heat-bath dynamics of µD , and that the ground state
is unique.
   A finite-size criterion (§3). By a Knabe-type argument for the heat-bath generator, which is
a frustration-free sum of projections, the heat-bath gap on every torus of sides at least ℓ + 1 is
bounded below as soon as the smallest heat-bath gap of an ℓ × ℓ box, over all placements and all
boundary conditions, exceeds 1/ℓ (Theorem 3.1).
   From a mixing estimate to box gaps (§4). After blocking 2 × 2 cells, µD becomes a translation-
invariant, nearest-neighbour model with a finite single-cell space and positive weights, to which the
theory of [10, 9] applies. We reproduce the coupling argument of [10, Proposition 2.1] in a form that
consumes only one estimate, a uniform bound below 1 on the total variation distance between the
laws of a shell segment Bi in a notched square Fi under two boundary conditions (Proposition 4.8).
With [10, Theorem 2.1] and [9, Theorem 4.1] this gives a uniform gap of the cell heat bath on large
squares, and hence the input of the finite-size criterion.
   The cut lemma (§5). The window law of Bi in Fi is a ratio of partition functions, and these
are the same for the frame model and the continuous model on S2 because every site has at most
four factors and the frames are 4-designs. Removing a closed cut X that separates Bi from the
boundary factorizes the partition function, and Lemma 5.3 shows that the window law differs from
a law that does not depend on the boundary condition by a factor e±2ε , where ε is the change of log
of a ratio of pinned and free partition functions under the soft removal of X. Removing X one site
at a time, ε is bounded by a sum over the cut of pair-correlation responses |Eτ [nu · nv ] − E0 [nu · nv ]|
at pairs of sites next to a removed site. Hypothesis (P), decay of these responses in the distance to
the pins, therefore implies the estimate of the previous step (Theorem 5.8).
   The pair recursion (§6–§7). Hypothesis (P) is proved by a walk expansion. A pair correlation is
a sum over trails from one site of the pair to the other, weighted by 3−|w| and by ratios of partition
functions of traced domains. The difference between the pinned and the free ratio telescopes over
              THE SPECTRAL GAP OF THE SPIN-2 AKLT MODEL ON THE SQUARE LATTICE                           3


the steps of the trail, and the response of each step is, exactly, a linear combination of pair responses
of the same type around the removed passage site. This closes into a linear recursion for the worst
pair response at distance d, with a nonnegative 2 × 2 kernel built from closed trails. The recursion
contracts if the per-step ratios are bounded by the constants cs , ct of (H1), if the weighted trail
sums decay geometrically (H2), and if the kernel has spectral radius below 1 (H3′ ) (Theorem 7.4).
   Computer-assisted inputs (§8). We state (H1), (H2) and (H3′ ) in the form used and describe
their certificates. The proofs for (H1), by positive definiteness of integer matrices and, for part of
the patterns, by closed forms, are in Appendix D; the tail estimate for (H3′ ) is in Appendix C.
   The logical dependence is therefore
(H1), (H2), (H3′ ) =⇒ (P) =⇒ window estimate =⇒ box gaps =⇒ heat-bath gap of µD =⇒ gap(HL,N ) ≥ c,
and the constants are chosen in this order. The third implication uses [10] at the level of its proof,
which is why that proof is reproduced in §4.
1.3. Conventions. Throughout, S2 is the unit sphere in R3 and dn is the normalized uniform
measure on it. On Z2 we write d(x, y) = |x1 − y1 | + |x2 − y2 | for the graph distance and |x − y|∞
for the ℓ∞ distance; x ∼ y means d(x, y) = 1. For probability measures ν, ν ′ on a finite set,
dTV (ν, ν ′ ) = supA |ν(A) − ν ′ (A)|. For a reversible Markov generator L on L2 (ν) with Dirichlet form
E, the spectral gap is gap = inf{E(F, F )/ Varν (F ) : F not constant}. All spin configurations are of
unit vectors; a “pin” is a unit vector attached to a boundary bond.

D.4. The computation, and what must be trusted. Patterns. The reflection x1 7→ −x1 maps
Pext onto itself, preserves the type of a step, permutes the slots, and leaves c(π) unchanged. The
list of patterns is produced by a computer enumeration of the rules of Definition 6.6, modulo this
reflection (the turns are stored with o = e) and modulo patterns that give the same functions
Zcur and Znext up to a common positive factor (the factor of the sites without present bonds,
Lemma D.2(1)). A second enumeration, written independently from Definition 6.6, finds 106,101
configurations that satisfy the rules and the same 7,962 classes, 2,696 straight and 5,266 turns,
class by class. In the representations of §D.1, the number of vectors is k ≤ 11.
   Programs. The program cert.py computes, for a pattern, a representation and an inversion set
I, the integer coefficients of 3DẐcur and 5DẐnext . It integrates out y by Lemma D.1 and contracts
the remaining sites as the ring e, (1, 1), N, (−1, 1), W, (−1, −1), s, (1, −1), with the exact moment
tensors of the site measures. All tensors in this contraction have nonnegative integer entries. The
contraction is carried out in 64-bit integer arithmetic, with run-time assertions that every entry
stays below 261 . The program then forms the matrices Aϵ , A′ϵ of §D.2: it changes the signs of
the coefficients according to I, substitutes the Pauli matrices with real and imaginary parts kept
separately, and passes to the basis of §D.2 for the pairs, again with assertions on the magnitudes
and with a fallback to unbounded integers. It checks exactly that the imaginary parts vanish, that
the matrices are symmetric, that the antisymmetric vector of every pair of a corner of status U is
annihilated from the left and from the right, and that no entry joins two basis vectors with different
eigenvalues of Σ3 or different exchange signs. Finally it tests Mϵ on Vϵ◦ , for every ϵ, by fraction-free
elimination in unbounded integers.
   The program closed exact.py carries out the pruning of §D.3 and the test of Proposition D.9
in rational arithmetic. The program run prod.py chooses the representation and I for each pat-
tern. It minimizes Ẑcur /Ẑnext numerically and lets I consist of the groups whose vectors have a
negative total component along the principal axis of the minimizer, using floating-point eigenvalues
to discard choices that cannot succeed. Floating-point arithmetic is used only for this choice. The
program verify all.py re-derives every certificate of a file from the stored data alone, without
any optimization.

the representation and I. The reduced representation is used for all patterns but two. The file
