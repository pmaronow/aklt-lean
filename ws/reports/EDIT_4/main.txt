  THE SPECTRAL GAP OF THE SPIN-2 AKLT MODEL ON THE SQUARE
                          LATTICE

        Abstract. We prove that the spin-2 AKLT Hamiltonian on the square-lattice torus has a unique
        ground state for every pair of integers L, N ≥ 3 and a spectral gap bounded below by one positive
        constant for all integers L, N ≥ L0 , for some fixed L0 , with either parity allowed for each period.
        The proof is computer-assisted. Its computational inputs are local operator inequalities, an open-
        trail growth bound, and a type-dependent positive-vector bound for a response kernel. These inputs
        are checked using exact matrix positivity certificates, a finite-memory test, and stationary Bellman
        inequalities with finite prefix sums. The theorem, including these finite checks, has been formalized
        in Lean 4 and checked by the Lean kernel. Three finite coherent-state frames compare the quantum
        gap with the gap of a finite-state heat-bath dynamics. A sign change on each lifted square permits
        arbitrary torus parities. A direct periodic block argument reduces the heat-bath gap to window
        estimates, which follow from a separating-cut factorization, a walk expansion, and a contracting
        response recursion. Uniqueness follows independently from polynomial divisibility.

dependent positive-vector bound obtained from stationary Bellman inequalities and finite prefix

1.1. Structure of the proof. The uniqueness assertion has a separate algebraic proof. The
kernel of each edge projection consists of multiples of its edge determinant. Unique factorization
combines these divisibilities, and degree four at every torus site leaves a scalar quotient. This
argument applies also when either period is three.
   For the gap, finite fourth moments in Section 2 give tight frames of coherent states. In Section 3
we assign three explicit rotated icosahedral frames according to a proper torus colouring. Their
separation makes the squared modulus of the frame image of BL,N strictly positive. The resulting
antiferromagnetic probability law on the torus has a single-site heat bath whose gap bounds the
quantum gap from below. A conditional physical square lifts to the plane, where a coordinatewise
sign change converts its law to the positive-interaction plane specification. This sign change is
defined on the lift.
   Section 4 uses a uniform estimate for window marginals to choose one square scale. A stopped
coupling bounds the Hamming cost of changing one exterior letter. Counting all periodic translates
of that square gives a gap for block updates, and comparison of conditional block and site Dirichlet
forms gives the torus site gap. Theorem 4.7 combines this with the quantum comparison of Theo-
rem 3.13 to obtain the uniform quantum gap. Section 5 proves the required window estimate from
the pair-response bound P(A, θ), uniformly over plane colourings and placements. Sections 6–8
prove that response bound using a walk expansion, a contracting positive-vector recursion, and the
exact computational inputs. Thus the gap implication is
                          exact computational inputs =⇒ P =⇒ W
                      =⇒ periodic block gap =⇒ site gap =⇒ quantum gap.

                                8. Exact computational inputs
   The computational inputs have three finite certificates. The local input tests integer Hermitian-
form matrices reconstructed from the covered patterns. The open-trail input tests a positive integer
vector for a memory-six one-step matrix. The kernel input tests five stationary lattice supersolu-
tions and their exact length-twelve prefixes. The certificates consist of the finite integer equalities,
inequalities, and divisibility tests specified below and in Appendix C, with positive rational de-
nominators cleared. The soundness arguments prove deterministic implications from these finite
assertions.
   Theorem 1.2, including the three inputs (H1), (H2), and (H3h ) and their certificates, has been
formalized in Lean 4 (version 4.35.0-rc2, with Mathlib) and checked by the Lean kernel. The
accompanying Lean formalization uses only the standard axioms propext, Classical.choice,
and Quot.sound, and it does not use native decide. The certificates are checked inside the Lean
kernel, not by external programs. The certificate for (H1) covers all 7962 local patterns and their
12173 sector blocks; its kernel check took about 139 CPU-hours. The certificate for (H3h ) consists
of 5035 generated modules; its kernel check took about 19.5 CPU-hours. The kernel check for
(H2) took seconds. Everything in the formalization except the certificates for (H1) and (H3h ),
in particular the derivation of Theorem 1.2 from the three inputs, has also been checked by two
